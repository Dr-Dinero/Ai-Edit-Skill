# Craft floor for council edits

The first council edit (C2690, Oct 2026) passed every technical check and the owner still
called it "edited horribly": one locked-off shot for 2:54, small closed-caption subtitles in a
box, no hook text, no structure, no sound. Clean joins and correct loudness are the minimum,
not the edit. Every talking-head Reel or Short now clears this floor before edit QC sees it.

## Picture

- **The frame changes on every jump cut.** A cut between two takes at the same framing is a
  visible jump. Alternate wide, medium, and tight crops of the full-resolution source so the
  cut reads as a punch-in.
- **No stretch over about 3.5 s without a framing change** in the teaching part. Long holds
  are allowed in a prayer, testimony, or other calm section (up to about 7 s), and even
  there the camera keeps a slow push.
- **Every shot moves.** A 3 to 7 % push or pull across the shot. A static frame reads as a
  webcam recording.
- **Punch in on the words that matter**: the claim, the named thing, the turn ("But here's the
  beautiful thing"), the command.
- **Tight is never tighter than mid-chest to head**, and the eyes sit about a third of the way
  down the frame. Crop from the 4K source, never upscale a 1080 proxy.
- A light, consistent grade (a touch of contrast and saturation, soft vignette) on the whole piece.

- **Show what he is talking about.** One locked-off speaker, however well cut, reads as
  average. Cut to licensed b-roll on the lines that name something visible (the bedroom at
  night, the phone, the chains, the light, the prayer) about every 10 to 15 s in teaching:
  full-frame for 1.3 to 2.6 s, or a top split that keeps his face on screen. Pick each clip
  by looking at it, start it on the moment that carries the meaning, and crop so the subject
  survives the vertical frame. Never cover a slam, and never let stock become the story.

## Words on screen

- **Captions are the main graphic, not subtitles.** 1 to 3 words per chunk, broken on phrase
  boundaries, all caps, heavy weight (about 90 px at 1080 wide), white with a thick dark
  outline and shadow, timed to the word (on screen 2 frames before it is spoken).
- **One accent color on the key words** (the topic, names, the promise). Restraint: not every
  line gets one.
- Caption chunks pop in quickly in the teaching part and fade in softly in a calm section.
- **Never over the mouth or chin**; sit them on the chest. Nothing in the top 8 %, bottom 20 %,
  or right 12 % (platform UI).

## Structure

- **Hook title on frame 1** (the frame is the thumbnail): the video's question in a few big
  words, held about 3 s, with headroom above the speaker.
- **Section chips** at each turn of the argument (what's happening, how it starts, how to get
  free, the prayer). They tell a scroller where they are and that more is coming.
- **Keyword slams** every 15 to 25 s: the frame dims for about a second and one spoken word lands
  big. Only words the speaker actually says. Captions step aside while a slam is up.
- **The CTA is on screen when it is said**, in the speaker's exact words.

## Sound

- Sound follows visible events, not every cut: a low hit under the hook title, an impact on
  each slam, a soft whoosh on each section chip, a gentle swell into a prayer, a small pop on
  the CTA. Effects sit clearly under the voice but are audible.
- A licensed music bed under the whole piece: tense and low through the teaching, changing to
  something warmer at the prayer. It sits about 12 dB under the voice and lifts in the gaps.
  Choose it by mood and listen to the join; a wrong or weak bed is worse than none.
- Cuts sit in real silence (the quietest 20 ms of the pause), with short fades so nothing clicks.
- Master to -14 LUFS, true peak at or below -1.5 dBTP, measured on the encoded file.

## Pacing

- Speech starts within 0.3 s. Pauses between phrases are trimmed to about a quarter second in
  teaching, about half a second in a prayer. Never cut inside a phrase and never speed up the voice.
- Remove retakes and false starts by phrase after reading them in context, and record why.

## What a script cannot judge

The renderer and craft QC enforce the floor. They cannot tell whether the hook is the best
line, whether a slam word is the right one, or whether a punch-in lands on a breath. Look at
the contact sheets and the opening frame by frame, and write what you could not check.
